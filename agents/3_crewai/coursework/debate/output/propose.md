I strongly support the motion that there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs.

LLMs are not ordinary tools. They can generate convincing falsehoods, imitate human speech at scale, spread misinformation instantly, enable fraud, and leak sensitive data. Left unregulated, the harms do not stay theoretical—they become systemic. A single powerful model can be copied, deployed, and misused millions of times, faster than any human institution can respond.

Strict laws are necessary for three reasons:

First, **public safety**. LLMs can be used to create scams, phishing messages, propaganda, and harmful instructions with alarming realism. Without legal standards, companies have too little incentive to prevent abuse before it happens.

Second, **accountability**. When an AI system causes harm, people deserve to know who is responsible, what data was used, and what safeguards were in place. Strict regulation would require transparency, audits, and clear liability instead of letting developers hide behind “the model did it.”

Third, **trust**. Society cannot rely on systems that are powerful but unaccountable. If people cannot trust AI outputs, then the technology becomes dangerous in education, healthcare, law, journalism, and public services. Regulation does not kill innovation; it makes innovation safe enough to be widely adopted.

The core principle is simple: the more powerful the technology, the stricter the rules should be. We already regulate cars, medicines, and airplanes because their potential benefits are huge, but so are their risks. LLMs deserve the same logic. Strong laws are not anti-progress—they are what make progress sustainable, ethical, and safe for everyone.
